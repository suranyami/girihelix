# Styles presentation — design (Plan 5 follow-up)

**Date:** 2026-10-24
**Status:** accepted — 2026-10-24
**Depends on:** Plan 5 (render styles — `Palette`, `Style`, the `:styles` exhibit). Plan 5 lives on `feat/render-styles`; implement this on a branch based on it, or on `main` once it has merged.
**Follows:** `2026-10-23-girihelix-render-styles-design.md` and the workbench design (`2026-10-16-girihelix-workbench-design.md`)

## Intent

Plan 5 made a constructed figure weave presentable, but the page is hard to inspect and the picture ends in a ragged patch. This follow-up makes the styles page readable as a tiling: the pattern fills a finite frame by repeating the unit cell on the lattice, the scaffolding tiles can be shown as a faint overlay, every face island is coloured, the canvas pans and zooms, and palettes are chosen from visible swatch rows instead of opaque numbers.

## Goals

- **Seamless finite patch.** The styles exhibit's picture fills its frame with the pattern's periodic repetition, showing two unit cells at rest — one for the heaviest tilings, whose construction would otherwise take tens of seconds — with no ragged edge and no bare margin at zoom 1. The content is generated by translating the unit cell on the lattice, so every internal cell seam is exact.
- **Every face island coloured.** `Faces.extract_faces/1` colours every separate face-adjacency island, not only the first, so every rosette gets its two-tone fill.
- **Tile overlay.** A `tiles` toggle lays each placed tile over the figure as a translucent overlay with a distinct faint tint per tile type, so the scaffolding is legible without hiding the design.
- **Pan and zoom on every canvas.** One client-side hook gives every workbench canvas drag-to-pan, pinch/ctrl-wheel zoom and a reset, with no page re-render per gesture.
- **Swatch palette picker.** The palette control is an inline, fixed-height scrollable list of Wada combinations, each row showing its colour blocks.

## Non-goals

- An infinite canvas or an SVG `<pattern>`; the patch is finite and pan/zoom is clamped.
- Colouring the tile mosaic as the subject; tiles are only a faint overlay above the figure, so the composition stays background → figure → tiles.
- Palette search or filtering by base colour; the list scrolls.
- Changing the three styles' draw vocabulary or the `Filled`/`Outline`/`Plain` geometry.
- A region-size parameter for `Instancing`; that stays on the backlog.

## Constraints

- **Engine purity.** `lib/girihelix/engine/**` stays standard-library only. The JS hook lives in `assets/js/` and the canvas component wires it.
- **Hard rules.** No `if` or `cond`; function heads and guards. Failing public functions return `{:error, reason}` with a bang twin. No new dependencies.
- **Ops are the render surface.** The serializer stays as it is; the overlay and the patch emit ordinary `Op.t()` values.
- **No JS inline in templates.** The hook is a module in the existing bundle, registered in `app.js`; templates carry only `phx-hook` and data attributes.
- **Faithful where it matters.** The styles' geometry is unchanged. The face-colouring extension is a deliberate divergence from Taprats and is documented as such.

## The seamless patch

The engine already builds a periodic design: `Construct` translates the same figure maps on `t1`/`t2`, so any two cells in the constructed map are exact translations of one another. The ragged picture comes from the viewport, not the geometry — `:fit` frames the whole constructed patch, including the fringe of whole figures whose cells merely intersect the region.

The fix is a **fixed viewport** framing the periodic interior of the patch:

- The catalogue's placements put the constructed content off the lattice origin, so the window is centred on the **drawing's content bounds**, not on the origin.
- The window is a fraction of those bounds, sized so it lies inside the region where every vertex has a `+t1` and a `+t2` counterpart. The shipped frame shows two unit cells at rest (one for a tiling above the vertex budget); the fraction follows from that count and the patch span.
- The styles exhibit pins one viewport for the whole run, not one per frame. The optional `viewport` callback gains an arity that receives the run's frames, so the exhibit takes the bounds from the final retained frame without constructing again and without raising on a failure frame. `:fit` stays the default for every other exhibit.
- The serializer's root `viewBox` is the fixed window. The SVG root is made overflow-visible and the canvas wrapper clips, so the ops outside the window are still in the markup and panning or zooming can reveal the fringe.
- The patch is enlarged beyond `Construct.default_region/1`: a span helper constructs a grid of unit cells large enough that the visible-cell window sits inside the periodic interior. The default build targets two cells (roughly a five-by-five-cell patch). **The span adapts to the tiling:** the exhibit measures the constructed one-cell map and, when it exceeds a vertex budget, builds one cell and shows one cell instead, because the heaviest catalogue tilings take tens of seconds at five cells. The exact budget is calibrated so `8.5` keeps two cells and `7.6`, `6.5 flower`, `3-8-4-6` and `Girih Star` use one.

Seamlessness is pinned by a property test, not by eye, for the two-cell windows of `8.5` and `6.5 flower`: every vertex of those windowed regions has a counterpart translated by `t1` and by `t2` within the constructed patch (rounded positions compared as sets). `7.6` does not hold this property at either span, and `3-8-4-6` misses at the one-cell span; both are recorded in `docs/backlog.md` rather than asserted.

## Face colouring: every island

`Faces.colour/1` currently seeds only the first kept face and drains its component. The change:

- After one component drains, the next `:undone` face in discovery order is seeded black and the propagation repeats, until no `:undone` face remains.
- Each island's first face is therefore black, matching the existing rule that an undecided seed falls through to black.
- `:filling_conflict` detection and the two-colour rule within a component are unchanged.
- The module doc records the divergence: Taprats colours only `faces[0]`'s component; girihelix colours every island so a flower tiling is not left with one painted rosette.

Goldens or tests that assume single-seed counts are updated; structural goldens (vertices, edges, verification) are unchanged.

## The tile overlay

The exhibit gains a `tiles` boolean parameter, default `false`.

- When on, every placed tile is appended after the figure ops: a `{:polygon, points, :fill, [fill: hex, opacity: 0.12]}` and a `{:polygon, points, :stroke, [stroke: hex, width: 0.75, opacity: 0.5]}` per placement.
- Tint per tile type: a fixed ordered list of five faint hues assigned by `feature_id` (modulo the list), so octagons and squares read differently and the assignment is stable across runs. The exact hues are the builder's call; they must stay legible in both themes.
- The overlay is generated from the construct state's `translations` and the tiling's placements: each placed feature's points are transformed by the placement, then by the lattice translation. It therefore covers the same patch as the figure.
- The overlay is drawn **after** the figure in every frame of the run, as a translucent layer on top. With the patch filling the frame, anything drawn beneath the figure would be hidden; the transparency keeps the design readable through the tiles. The style's background is still returned separately so the composition is explicit: **background → figure → tiles**.
- Frame zero has no lattice translations yet; the overlay then draws the tiling's in-cell placements at the identity translation.
- Existing tile outlines remain part of the structure drawing; the overlay's fill and stroke are added alongside them, not in place of them.

## Pan and zoom

One hook, `CanvasZoom`, in `assets/js/`, registered in `app.js`, attached by the canvas component's wrapper on every exhibit.

- **Pan:** pointer drag moves the content; the wrapper clips overflow.
- **Zoom:** pinch on touch, or ctrl/⌘-wheel on a trackpad/mouse, zooms about the pointer. Plain wheel scrolls the page and never traps it.
- **Controls:** small `+`, `−` and reset buttons in the canvas corner, so zoom is reachable without a gesture.
- **Clamps:** scale between roughly 0.6 and 8. Translation is not clamped for v1, so panning can move into blank space; the clamp is recorded in the backlog.
- **Lifecycle:** the transform survives frame and parameter updates (LiveView patches the SVG inside the hook's wrapper, and the hook re-applies its styles after each patch); reset returns to the fitted view. Navigating to another exhibit reuses the same `#canvas`, so a changed `data-canvas-key` resets the view instead of remounting.
- The SVG remains server-rendered and unchanged; the hook only transforms the wrapper's content with CSS.

## Palette roles and derived tones

The content declares the roles it needs, and the palette maps a combination onto them:

- `Filled` needs `edge`, `darker` and `lighter`; it has no background.
- `Plain` and `Outline` need `edge` and `background`.
- The tile overlay keeps its fixed faint tints and does not consume palette roles.

Mapping: sort the combination by luminance; the darkest colour is the edge and the lightest is the background where the style needs one; the two faces take the most separated pair among the remaining colours. When the combination has fewer colours than the roles need, the missing tones are **derived from a face colour by shifting HSL lightness** with hue and saturation kept, clamped at the ends, rather than by recycling a colour. A two-colour combination becomes edge + one face + a derived second face; a one-colour combination becomes a mono-hue weave of derived light and dark tones.

`Palette.roles/2` takes the combination and the role list. Each style declares the roles it needs, the exhibit resolves the palette once into that role map, and `Style.paint/3` still receives a plain role map.

## The palette picker

`Param` gains a `:palette` type. It decodes, encodes and takes extremes exactly like `:enum`; its options are the 348 combination atoms.

The controls component renders a `:palette` parameter as:

- A fixed-height, independently scrollable list (about 18rem tall) inside the controls column — not a floating popover.
- One row per combination: the combination number, then its 3–5 colour blocks as inline swatches.
- Each row is a radio input inside the existing `<form phx-change="params">`, so selection uses the same URL/patch pipeline as every other parameter. The selected row is visually marked.
- No search field and no lazy loading for v1; 348 rows is acceptable. If it renders slowly, windowing is a follow-up.

## Testing

- **Seam:** the periodicity property test above, for at least the default tiling and one flower tiling (`6.5 flower`).
- **Faces:** a hand-built map with two disconnected triangles colours both islands; a conflicting component still returns `:filling_conflict`; existing face goldens updated where the island rule changes counts.
- **Overlay:** off by default; on appends one fill per placed tile at every lattice translation, with distinct fills per `feature_id`, after the figure ops, so the composition is background → figure → tiles.
- **Viewport:** fixed, centred, contained inside the patch bounds; not `:fit`.
- **Palette:** `:palette` round-trips through `Param.decode/2` and `Param.encode/2`; the controls render one row per combination with the right number of swatches; selecting one updates the exhibit.
- **Canvas:** the component test asserts the hook attribute and the zoom/reset controls; drag/pinch behaviour is checked manually in the browser.
- **Registry:** the `tiles` extremes run and every frame still draws non-empty ops and rows.
- **Performance:** measure construction and rendering for the largest catalogue tilings at each adaptive span, and the styles exhibit's share of the registry property test. The vertex budget is the guard: heavy tilings show one cell to keep the cost down, though the heaviest, `3-8-4-6`, still takes about 87 seconds at one cell, and a construction-cache or weld-scaling improvement is a recorded follow-up.

## Decisions taken (our calls, for review)

1. The seamless picture is a content-centred fixed viewport inside an enlarged, lattice-built patch — not op repetition or an SVG pattern. The patch span and the visible-cell count are two by default and one for tilings above the measured vertex budget. The optional `viewport` callback gains an arity receiving the run's frames so the pin needs no second construction.
2. Every face island is seeded; each island starts black. This deliberately diverges from Taprats' single seed.
3. The tile overlay uses five fixed faint hues cycled by feature id, at 0.12 opacity, drawn **over** the figure; the style background is returned separately so the composition is background → figure → tiles; frame zero uses the identity translation.
4. One canvas hook serves every exhibit; gestures plus buttons, with clamped scale and no page-scroll trapping. The SVG root is overflow-visible and the wrapper clips, so panning can reveal the constructed patch's fringe.
5. The palette picker is an inline scrollable radio list in the controls column, not a popover, with no search for v1.
6. Palette roles are declared by content and missing tones are derived by shifting lightness; no colour is recycled.
7. No new dependencies and no change to the serializer's draw-op vocabulary; the root class and overflow are presentation only.

## Implementation record

The calls the draft left open, as shipped.

- **The adaptive span is measured, not guessed.** `Run.build/4` probes every run at one cell and measures the probe's final map with `PlanarMap.count_vertices/1`. Above `@vertex_budget 2_000` the probe *is* the run and its frames are kept; otherwise the probe is discarded and the exhibit runs again at two cells. The budget was calibrated with the rosette binder on one-cell `patch_region/2` maps, which are three-by-three patches: `8.5` 1,392 vertices, `7.6` 4,321 and `6.5 flower` 6,164. `8.5` therefore keeps two cells, while `7.6`, `6.5 flower`, `3-8-4-6` and `Girih Star` drop to one. A probe that raises proves nothing about the span, so it is repeated at two cells and that run's failure is reported.
- **Visible cells stay at two, and no three-cell window shipped.** The visible window is sized by the same `cells` value that sizes the patch region, so a wider window multiplies the same construction cost. Task 4 measured that the patch cost already made the largest tiling impractical at five cells; the fix is the adaptive span plus the construction scaling noted in `docs/backlog.md`, which also defers a three-cell window until that cost is addressed.
- **The viewport formula.** `Styles.viewport/2` takes the last frame's state, `cell = max(|t1|, |t2|)`, and pins a window of `cells * cell / @canvas_aspect` map units (`@canvas_aspect 0.75`) at scale `@nominal_width / width` (`@nominal_width 1000.0`), centred on the constructed map's vertex bounds. An empty map falls back to the structure ops' bounds; if neither yields a centre, the result is `Viewport.fit(nil)`. The pinned viewport is computed once per run from the frames in hand.
- **A run is a task; a frame is not.** `ExhibitLive` builds with `start_async/3` and reports progress per step through `Steps.run/3`'s `:on_step`. A frame navigation reuses the frames already assigned through `Run.view/4`; only a change to `{slug, values}` starts a new run, and a superseded run is cancelled.
- **Wheel zoom normalises `deltaMode`.** A wheel delta arrives in pixels (`0`), lines (`1`) or pages (`2`); lines are multiplied by 16 px and pages by the canvas height before `Math.exp(-delta * 0.002)`, so ctrl/⌘-wheel zooms in Firefox's line mode rather than being dead.
- **The star skip follows the side count.** `Instancing.star_skip/1` returns `3` for three or four sides and `div(sides - 1, 2)` otherwise, so a hexagon is a hexagram rather than three diameters through its centre while the square keeps its cross-shaped star. The binder passes `star_skip(sides) * 1.0`, keeping `Star.build!/3`'s float contract.
- **Derived tone constants.** A missing palette tone shifts HSL lightness by `0.18` with hue and saturation kept, clamped to `[0.08, 0.92]`; a shift the clamp leaves unchanged is reversed.
- **Overlay tints.** Five fixed hues — `#2563eb`, `#d97706`, `#0d9488`, `#db2777`, `#7c3aed` — cycled by `feature_id`, with fill opacity `0.12`, stroke opacity `0.5` and stroke width `0.75`.
- **Pan clamping and delegated button listeners are deferred.** `CanvasZoom` clamps scale to `[0.6, 8.0]` only; pan translation is not clamped into the content's bounds, and the zoom buttons are bound per element at mount, so a button added by a later LiveView patch would be inert. Both are recorded in `docs/backlog.md`.
- **The hook's state survives patches; navigation is keyed.** LiveView's morphdom writes the server tree's attributes back over `#canvas` and `[data-zoom-content]`, dropping the `transform` and `touch-action` the hook sets, so `updated()` restores both after every patch. `#canvas` is morphed in place across exhibits rather than remounted, so the canvas component stamps it with `data-canvas-key` (the exhibit slug); `updated()` resets scale, translation and pointer state when the key changes, which is what makes a navigation start at the fitted view.
- **The seam property is checked on rounded positions.** The test compares six-decimal rounded vertex positions, translating the exact position and rounding once; translating an already-rounded position double-rounds and can miss a counterpart that is present.
